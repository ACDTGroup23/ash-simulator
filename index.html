    Our measurements

    Barley grew in two 12-cell trays. Each column got 0, 0.17, 0.46 or 0.99 g of ash per cell, a quarter of the protocol amounts for low, high and extreme fires (0.69, 1.84 and 3.95 g). Set 1 was sown on 23 Oct and Set 2 on 26 Oct. Bean did not germinate even in the controls, so it has no data.

    Ash level did not change barley height (two-way ANOVA on the 46 plants of 6 Nov: ash p = 0.47, set p < 0.001). Set 2 was 3.2 cm shorter than Set 1.

    Barley, second trial in pots

    Sown on 11 Nov in four 11 cm pots, 3 seeds each. The pots got the same ash as the bean second trial: 0, 5.70, 15.11 and 32.49 g, which equal 0, 6.0, 15.9 and 34.2 Mg/ha.

    At least 2 cm, because the seeds were sown 2 cm deep. The real depth was not measured.

    The no-ash and low pots had no seedlings, so there is no control to compare with.

    Before vs after the experiment

    Before the experiment we only had published values, and two things were unknown: how salty our ash is and how much of its salt reaches the roots (f). In the model they always appear multiplied together, so we follow their product, the effective ash EC.

    After the experiment, we compare the barley heights with the model at the ash we really added: 0.17, 0.46 and 0.99 g per cell, which equal 1.5, 4.0 and 8.5 Mg/ha. Effective ash EC values that disagree with the heights are ruled out, and the model is run again.
